**An AI avatar is a synthetic on-screen presenter, built from footage or a photo of a person or from a licensed stock actor, that delivers any script you type with matching lip movement, voice and gestures.**

Vendors describe the same idea in their own terms. Descript calls it "an animated on-screen presenter to deliver your script." HeyGen's digital twin "is trained from footage of a real person, and clones a voice from the same recording so the twin speaks in the subject's own voice," while its photo avatar "is created from a single still image of a person." Synthesia's photo avatars use "a speech-driven animation model rather than motion learned from recorded footage." Studio avatars and real-time interactive avatars complete the range.

The format suits talking-head video that needs frequent updates or many languages, such as training and explainers. [HeyGen](/tools/heygen) pairs avatars with video translation and lip-sync, and [Synthesia](/tools/synthesia) pairs them with AI dubbing and SCORM export for learning platforms. The voice is usually [text-to-speech](/glossary/text-to-speech) or a [cloned voice](/glossary/voice-cloning).

Consent is the safeguard that matters most. HeyGen requires a consent video, recorded by the person represented, for every video-based digital twin. For a personal avatar made from a photo, Synthesia requires a live consent recording from the person in the photo, which "cannot be uploaded." In its web app, D-ID checks a webcam recording with a spoken passcode against the avatar's face and voice; through its API, an uploaded recording of the consent script is accepted instead. The exceptions are documented as well: HeyGen runs no consent check for photo avatars, leaving the subject's agreement to you, and waives consent collection for Enterprise accounts that have signed an indemnity agreement.
