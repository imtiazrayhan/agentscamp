Evaluate Napkin AI when the task is turning a short, approved explanation into one visual that an owner can inspect before reuse. Start with the relationship to communicate, then judge whether the draft preserves it. A useful result is a reviewed explanation and its actual export, with the source paragraph retained beside the approval record.

The [product site](https://www.napkin.ai/) describes imported or pasted text becoming editable visuals. Its listed exports include PPT, PNG, PDF and SVG, subject to plan. That describes a drafting route; it does not establish that any generated arrow, label or quantity is supported by the source.

## A proposed one-visual test

Use the fictional refill-station brief from the [explanatory diagram workflow](/guides/design/ai-explanatory-diagram-from-text). Visitors can bring a container or borrow one at the desk. Both options use the dispenser, then return to the desk for a refill label. The brief supplies neither usage percentages nor recurring visits.

Prepare a label table and request two equal option columns with shared instructions. This is a suggested evaluation, not a report of a tested Napkin result. Compare every label with the approved text and explain every connector. Reject a circular layout that turns returning to the desk into repeated participation, or an icon that implies a measured share.

A useful correction case is a draft that changes “borrow at the desk” to “free container.” The source has not stated a price. Restore the supplied action and record why the new claim was removed. The review should remain about meaning before it becomes about illustration style.

## Check the export and handoff

Inspect the export at the size intended for a slide or document. Check that both options still point to the shared instructions, the labels remain readable and no connector has shifted into an ambiguous position. Retain the editable version where available, plus a text equivalent and the approved paragraph version.

Ask the person who will reuse the visual to locate the source for one label and explain what an arrow means. If they cannot, include a caption or companion evidence table. The tool output alone should not carry the entire review record. A future editor needs to know which relationships were deliberate and which additions were rejected.

## Plan and workflow fit

The [pricing page](https://www.napkin.ai/pricing/) places PNG and PDF exports in the free plan, with PPT and SVG exports on paid plans. Check the actual account and delivery requirement before assuming the chosen output will remain editable downstream. This profile does not quote a price or promise identical behavior in every receiving application.

Use a [source-grounded mind map](/guides/workflow/source-grounded-ai-mind-map) when you first need to organize several documents and unresolved concepts. Compare [Miro](/tools/miro) if the reviewing team needs an evolving canvas for relationships. Napkin fits a bounded explanation when the owner can verify its labels, correct misleading visual conventions and review the delivered export. It does not replace evidence review or whole-presentation planning.
