Gamma is the tool for the deck you needed an hour ago. You give it a prompt, an outline, or an existing document, and it produces a card-based presentation, a document, a website, or a set of social posts with a theme applied and images generated. Gamma's own description is "your free-to-use AI design partner for creating effortless presentations, websites, and more."

Since Gamma 3.0 (September 15, 2025) the center of the product is Gamma Agent, a chat collaborator that can research a topic on the web with citations, restyle an entire deck, build diagrams, understand screenshots and links, and make project-wide edits. For marketers that turns Gamma from a slide generator into a place to draft the launch deck, the one-pager, and the landing page from the same source material.

## Highlights

- **Four output formats.** Presentations, documents, websites, and social posts from one editor, and any gamma can be converted into a published site with multi-page navigation.
- **Gamma Agent.** Natural-language edits across the whole project: research with citations, redesign, diagrams, and brand restyling.
- **Exports that survive the handoff.** PDF, PNG, and PowerPoint (PPTX), plus direct posting to LinkedIn. Google Slides users import the PPTX.
- **Publishing.** Every account gets a gamma.site subdomain; Pro adds custom domains for up to 10 multi-page sites, Ultra 100, Business 100 with identity-provider sign-in.
- **API and connectors.** API key access is on Pro, Ultra, Teams, and Business, billed in credits; Gamma's ChatGPT and Claude connectors work on all plans, including Free and Plus.
- **Credits with rollover.** Paid plans refill monthly, unused credits roll over up to double the plan size, and Gamma does not charge credits for AI errors or clarification requests.

## In a marketer's workflow

Gamma is strongest as the last mile: the messaging, facts, and structure already exist, and you need a designed artifact. Keep the source of truth in your own files and let an agent produce the outline Gamma consumes.

```text
From ./launch/messaging.md and ./launch/faq.md, write a 10-slide
outline for a customer webinar deck: one line per slide title,
3-4 bullets each, a speaker note under each slide, no claims that
are not in the source files. Save as ./launch/webinar-outline.md.
```

Paste the outline into Gamma's create flow, pick the brand theme, and use Agent to tighten anything that reads like a bullet list. The same outline becomes a document for the sales team and a short site for the registration page. The [Claude Code for marketers](/guides/marketing/claude-code-for-marketers) guide covers keeping messaging files in a repo so every deck starts from the same facts, and the [content repurposer](/skills/marketing/content-repurposer) skill handles the outline-to-format step.

> [!TIP]
> Generation is metered per slide and per image. Import a finished outline rather than asking Gamma to write from a one-line prompt; you spend credits on layout and images, not on drafting text you will rewrite anyway.

## Good to know

Gamma is a hosted web app with no desktop client. Plans are Free, Plus, Pro, Ultra, and Business, with Enterprise for larger organizations. Gamma's help center lists the credit allowances: 400 one-time credits on Free, 1,000 a month on Plus, 4,000 on Pro, 20,000 on Ultra, and 6,000 per member on Business, which the help center prices at $40 per member per month billed annually as of September 2026. Plus, Pro, and Ultra are individual subscriptions priced on gamma.app, with annual billing at a 28 percent discount and the same monthly credits as monthly billing. Free exports carry a Made by Gamma badge; Plus and above remove it. Ultra and Business include every image model Gamma supports, and Business locks the never-train-on-your-data setting and offers SOC 2 documentation on request.

The trade-off is that a Gamma lives in Gamma. PPTX export is good, but the design system, analytics, and sites are the platform's, so a team that wants artifacts as files it owns should compare [Claude Design](/tools/claude-design).
