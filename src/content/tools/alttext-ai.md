AltText.ai is a drafting option for teams processing image alternatives through a repeatable publishing workflow. Its [support information](https://alttext.ai/support) describes web, CMS, and API use, with supported formats varying. Evaluate the draft quality and review process together: a fluent sentence does not establish that it serves the image’s purpose on your page.

Start with [alternative text](/glossary/alternative-text) as a contextual editorial decision. The relevant question is what the reader needs at this placement, using evidence from actual image inspection. Keep that decision in the pilot packet so corrections can be assessed against the owner’s stated purpose.

## A proposed purpose-sensitive evaluation

This evaluation uses fictional observations, not a hands-on AltText.ai result. The packet describes a refill station with a tap and three bottle slots. It supplies the asset version, the actual page excerpt, and two uses: an informative article and a decorative banner.

Use the [alt-text context review workflow](/guides/design/ai-alt-text-context-review) to record those uses before drafting. Compare the returned proposals against the observation packet. Can the reviewer identify which included details are supported? Does the wording add a claim about water quality, universal accessibility, or people’s intentions that the packet never established? Record each unsupported claim and the human correction.

For the article, the owner may approve a short description of the pictured station. For each placement, record the owner’s decision and the reason before accepting the wording. [W3C’s decision tree](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/images/decision-tree/) distinguishes meaningful, decorative, and functional uses. Do not apply one generated string to every occurrence simply because the asset ID is shared.

Inspect the implementation path in your own environment. Determine where drafts appear, who reviews them, how approved edits are saved, and what happens when an image is replaced. These are acceptance questions for your pilot, not implied behavior of every integration. Keep any automatic publication setting outside the evaluation until the owner approves the review process.

## Credit and format costs

[The pricing page](https://assets.alttext.ai/pricing) lists trial credits, recurring plans, and one-time packs, with different credit requirements for standard and advanced formats. Confirm the current requirements for your actual image types and workflow before estimating cost. This profile treats the service as paid rather than presenting trial credits as an ongoing free plan.

Include reviewer time in that estimate. A batch with many unsupported drafts may take longer to repair than a smaller set drafted from inspected observations. Measure the correction categories in your pilot rather than adopting a general quality or SEO-uplift claim.

## Fit and limits

AltText.ai is worth evaluating when a team has enough images to benefit from batch drafting and a named person to approve each placement’s wording. It does not replace evidence collection or page-context review. Its output does not establish accessibility conformance.

For charts and other dense visuals, use the [long-description workflow](/guides/design/ai-chart-long-descriptions). Finish the pilot with accepted examples, correction notes, unresolved cases, and an implementation check so the publishing owner can decide whether the workflow is useful.
