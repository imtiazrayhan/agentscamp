Surfer is the content optimization tool most SEO teams have used at some point: paste a keyword, and the Content Editor builds guidelines from the pages already ranking, then scores your draft live as you write. In 2026 the company describes itself as an "AI Visibility Platform" and has added tracking for how ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, and Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode mention your brand, which is the part that matters as search shifts toward answers.

For a content team the product is a loop: research the topic, write against the guidelines, publish through an integration, audit what slipped, and now watch whether AI answers cite you. Surfer says it is "100% made and hosted in Europe."

## Highlights

- **Content Editor with live scoring.** A Content Score and term, heading, and length guidelines update as you type. Every plan includes AI SEO optimization, the Content Score, a plagiarism check, and an AI detector with a Humanizer.
- **Surfy, the AI writing assistant.** Built into the editor on all plans for drafting and rewriting inside the guidelines rather than in a separate chat.
- **Content Audit and Topical Map.** Audit finds underperforming pages and Topical Map plans clusters. Standard lists rank drop detection; Pro adds a cannibalization report and 1-click internal linking.
- **AI Tracker.** Track a set of AI prompts (25 weekly on Standard, 50 daily on Pro, 100 daily on Peace of Mind) and see mention gaps and brand sentiment across AI models.
- **Integrations.** WordPress, Google Docs, Contentful, and Zapier on Standard and up, plus a Chrome extension; Peace of Mind includes API access.
- **MCP on Pro and above.** Surfer lists MCP among Pro, Peace of Mind, and AI Search Analytics features, so its data can be reached from an agent.

## In a content team's workflow

The practical split is to let Surfer own the ranking signal and let an agent own the text and the files. A typical brief-to-draft cycle: generate the Content Editor guidelines for the target keyword, export or copy the term list and headings, and hand them to Claude Code alongside your brand files.

```text
Here are the Surfer guidelines for "email deliverability checklist":
target 1,800-2,200 words, H2s to cover, and the term list with counts.
Draft the article from ./briefs/deliverability.md in our voice
(./brand/voice.md). Hit every H2, use each term naturally within its
range, and do not invent statistics; leave a [SOURCE NEEDED] marker instead.
```

Paste the draft back into the Content Editor, fix what the score flags, and publish through the Google Docs or WordPress integration. The [SEO content workflow with Claude Code](/guides/marketing/seo-content-workflow-with-claude-code) guide turns this into a repeatable command, and the [Claude Code for marketers](/guides/marketing/claude-code-for-marketers) pillar covers the setup.

> [!TIP]
> Treat term counts as a ceiling, not a target. The score rewards coverage, and an agent will happily stuff every term if you ask it to "maximize the score." Ask for natural use within range and check the Humanizer only as a last pass.

## Good to know

Surfer is a hosted web app with a Chrome extension. As of September 2026 the pricing page shows per-month prices on yearly billing: Discovery $49 (create or optimize 120 documents, track 10 pages, 1 seat), Standard $99 (360 documents, 3 seats, integrations, 25 AI prompts refreshed weekly), Pro $182 (360 documents, 5 seats, 5 Brand Spaces, 50 daily AI prompts, internal linking, MCP), and Peace of Mind $299 (unlimited documents, 10 seats, 100 daily prompts, API access, dedicated success manager). A tracking-only AI Search Analytics plan is $82, and Enterprise starts at $999 with SSO, white-label, and an advisory program. Monthly billing exists by card; Surfer's FAQ says annual saves up to 17 percent and that on annual plans credits reset yearly, so the document counts above are a yearly pool.

Document caps are the thing to watch: a team publishing daily will hit 360 documents well inside a year. If you want unlimited users and no document math, [Clearscope](/tools/clearscope) is the usual alternative, and the [Surfer vs Clearscope](/guides/comparisons/surfer-vs-clearscope) guide sets out where each wins.
