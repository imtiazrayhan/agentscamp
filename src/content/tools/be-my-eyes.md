Be My Eyes offers a personal visual-assistance workflow rather than a publisher’s image-metadata pipeline. Its [Be My AI instructions](https://support.bemyeyes.com/hc/en-us/articles/18133134809105-How-do-I-use-Be-My-AI) describe picture descriptions, follow-up questions or photos, and access to sighted volunteers. Explore the service around the person’s immediate question, then verify any consequential detail with appropriate evidence.

The [download page](https://www.bemyeyes.com/download-app/) lists free personal access on iOS, Android, Windows, and macOS; desktop access is for personal use. Check the current access requirements for the device you intend to use. Picture assistance depends on internet access, so include that condition in the practical evaluation.

## A proposed chart-assistance example

This example is fictional and describes a possible evaluation, not a hands-on result. A person has a picture of a workshop chart with bars labeled “Planned” and “Held.” They ask what the chart shows, then request more detail about the labels and units. If the picture is unclear, a follow-up photo or a volunteer interaction may help address the immediate question.

Keep the answer’s evidence level visible. A description may help someone explore the pictured relationships, but it does not make every numerical statement correct. In the fictional chart, the original table has eight planned sessions and six held sessions. Check those values against the table rather than accepting a guessed bar height.

If an answer calls the planned sessions delivered, preserve the mismatch and return to the source labels. If it proposes a cause for the gap, ask what supplied evidence supports that explanation. The useful next step may be obtaining the original table, not asking for a more confident version of the same inference.

## Personal assistance and publication are different jobs

A publisher who needs a chart’s durable text equivalent should use the [chart long-description workflow](/guides/design/ai-chart-long-descriptions). That process registers the chart version, verifies values, and checks how the description is associated with the page. Be My Eyes should not be presented as a CMS publishing service or an accessibility-conformance audit.

For ordinary still images on a page, use [alt-text review in context](/guides/design/ai-alt-text-context-review). The publisher must decide the image’s role and approve wording for that placement. [W3C’s image guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/images/decision-tree/) makes purpose relevant to that choice; a personal conversation about a picture does not establish the page’s editorial intent.

## What to evaluate

Test a harmless everyday question, how useful follow-up clarification is, and whether human assistance addresses uncertainty that remains. Record the device and access conditions alongside the observed result. This profile does not report a trial or guarantee that any specific picture will be described correctly.

Do not treat visual assistance as proof of a person’s identity, medical condition, product safety, or permission to act. Those conclusions need evidence beyond a pictured appearance. Choose [AltText.ai](/tools/alttext-ai) for comparison when the actual job is integrating description drafts into a publishing process, and evaluate its separate human review requirements.

Be My Eyes is useful to consider for personal picture exploration and questions with a clear uncertainty boundary. The final judgment should follow the user’s verified information and any appropriate human clarification, rather than the fluency of an AI answer.
