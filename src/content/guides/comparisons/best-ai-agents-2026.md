**The best AI agent in 2026 is the one that already sits where your work happens.** For documents, files and research, start with [Claude](/tools/claude), which now includes Cowork, or [ChatGPT](/tools/chatgpt) Work. For one standalone agent that works in a cloud sandbox, your browser and your own computer, [Manus](/tools/manus) is the most flexible. For actions across your business apps, use [Lindy](/tools/lindy) or AI by Zapier. On Microsoft 365, Copilot Cowork is the in-tenant choice. Two 2026 changes that older lists miss: ChatGPT agent is gone, replaced by ChatGPT Work and its cloud browser, and Claude Cowork is now part of Claude.

This roundup covers agents that take a multi-step task from a knowledge worker and finish it. Coding agents are in the [best Claude Code alternatives](/guides/comparisons/best-claude-code-alternatives-2026). Each tool page lists current plans with dates.

*Last reviewed: November 2026.*

## Manus: one agent, several places to run

Manus plans a whole job in its own cloud and runs it wherever you allow: a cloud sandbox by default, a paid Cloud Computer that "stays on when your laptop is closed", your own Chrome or Edge through the Browser Operator extension, or your Apple Silicon Mac or Windows PC through the Manus Studio app. Wide Research "deploys hundreds of independent agents that work in parallel", and since Manus 2.0 (September 28, 2026) Automations can start a task from a new email, a calendar event, a Slack message or a Notion update. Meta acquired Manus on December 29, 2025; Manus says it "formally resumed independent operations" on September 1, 2026.

Browser Operator requires you to "authorize every session", and closing the tab stops it. Studio's default permission mode auto-approves safe local actions and blocks high-risk ones such as sending private keys to the internet. The documented weak spots: in the browser, complex interactions "such as drag-and-drop and multi-step forms, may not work perfectly", and Studio's approval modes "limit changes, not reading".

**Verdict:** the most flexible standalone agent, if you are willing to configure each surface.

## Claude: Cowork is now just Claude

"Claude Cowork is now just Claude." Starting September 16, 2026, chat and Cowork began merging into one Claude, and "when a task calls for working with your files, Claude brings in Cowork's capabilities automatically." It reached general availability for Enterprise on October 23, 2026 and for Team on November 12, 2026. You can "start a task from your phone, and Claude keeps working in the cloud even when your laptop is closed", while the desktop app reaches "the folders and applications on your own computer". Claude uses connectors first, your browser when needed, and your screen "as a last resort"; computer use is in research preview as of November 2026.

"By default, Claude asks before taking an action", "Deleting anything needs your approval", and a permissions setting makes Claude show its plan and wait before anything significant. The documented cost: "agentic tasks consume more capacity than regular chat." The [Claude Cowork page](/tools/claude-cowork) has the history and plugins.

**Verdict:** the best fit when the work is files, documents and connected business apps, and you want one assistant for chat and delegation.

## ChatGPT Work and the cloud browser (ChatGPT agent is retired)

OpenAI's help article now starts with "ChatGPT agent is no longer available. Use ChatGPT Work for longer, multi-step tasks and finished deliverables." The Operator website is gone too. Work researches, analyzes, and produces documents, spreadsheets, presentations, reports or Sites, and can run once, on a schedule or trigger, or watch for changes. "Work on web and mobile runs in the cloud"; the desktop app can use local files and apps with your permission where your plan and workspace allow. The cloud browser "gives ChatGPT Work its own browser on a separate computer in the cloud", on paid plans in supported regions, excluding Free and Go.

By default ChatGPT asks before opening a new website in the cloud browser, and it requests confirmation before hard-to-reverse actions such as confirming a booking or making a payment. Credentials you type there "are not visible to the model." The documented limit: "Some websites use security measures that restrict access from automated browser agents."

**Verdict:** the natural choice for ChatGPT users who want finished deliverables, with the cloud browser for web steps.

## Perplexity Comet and Perplexity Computer

[Comet](/tools/perplexity-comet) is Perplexity's browser, and its Assistant "runs alongside your main browser window in a panel": "You can freely browse other pages while Assistant is doing the work. And run as many errands in parallel as you want." It runs in your own browser with your logged-in sessions. You can watch each step and pause it, which matters, because researchers have published several [prompt injection](/glossary/prompt-injection) attacks against its agent.

Perplexity Computer, on [Perplexity](/tools/perplexity)'s paid plans, is "an independent digital worker that completes tasks and workflows for you." It "normally runs in a secure cloud sandbox", with a Personal Computer option for Mac and Windows files and apps. It monitors email, calendar, flight status and files with condition-based triggers, runs sub-agents and parallel web research, and connects to Gmail, Slack, Notion, Salesforce and more. Limit what you connect, because "Computer doesn't undo work it already did in a connected app, such as edits to an online store."

**Verdict:** Comet for read-mostly work across tabs you opened yourself; Computer for recurring monitoring and research jobs in the cloud.

## Lindy: the AI employee in Slack

Lindy calls itself "the AI employee that plugs into your tools, learns how you work, and takes real work off your plate", and works "in Slack, email, text and your calendar, with 3,000+ integrations + MCP", and uses "phone calls and a browser for everything else". The control is the clearest in this list: "Lookups just happen. Anything with outside impact waits for your OK, until you tell me to go ahead." The documented limit is credits: "If the pool runs low, Lindy pauses and tells you."

**Verdict:** the pick for a Slack-first team that wants one assistant with company context.

## Zapier Agents, moving into AI by Zapier

[Zapier Agents](/tools/zapier-agents) runs agents across "9,000+ apps" on Zapier's platform, but it is in transition: "Zapier is migrating Agents (agents.zapier.com) to AI by Zapier, a more powerful AI experience built directly into the Zap editor," and "Zapier has not set a date to turn off Agents yet." AI by Zapier is an agentic step inside a Zap, billed in tasks.

AI by Zapier has the stronger controls: per-tool "Require approval before running", a pause for review when a run exceeds its task limit (75 by default, adjustable up to 500), and an admin option to require approval before Zaps with AI steps publish. In legacy Agents, approval comes from your instructions or a Human in the Loop step. The documented gaps: on-demand agents "do not have a direct equivalent trigger in the Zap editor yet", and knowledge sources of 1,000 or more pages or files can time out.

**Verdict:** right if your company already runs on Zaps; build new work in AI by Zapier.

## Genspark Super Agent

Genspark's Super Agent "takes a whole job. You describe the finished thing and add the files. It plans the steps, runs them on a cloud computer, and hands back files you can open." New Super Agent gives each project its own cloud computer, while Classic mode has none, and you can save a workflow as a skill to run again. For control, new images, video and music are confirm-first: "The agent asks one short form before generating anything." The limits: "A long job can run for up to 2 hours", and Genspark warns that long chats, retries and big files cost more than its estimates suggest.

**Verdict:** a strong cloud option for jobs that end in files, though each turn stops at two hours and you have to ask it to continue.

## Tasks in Gemini

Google's agent is Tasks in Gemini: "flip the toggle from 'Chat' to 'Tasks' and hand your busywork off to Gemini." It is free for users 18 and over in the United States, Canada, Australia, India, Japan and Korea, and for Pro and Ultra subscribers in select countries. It needs a personal Google account: "For now, this feature isn't available if you sign in with a work or school Google Account." To use websites, "Gemini can connect to the local Chrome browser on your desktop or use a separate remote browser in the cloud", and for steps such as signing in, the task pauses until you take over. The documented limit: "If a schedule runs when you are offline, you may not be able to stop Gemini from completing an unintended action." See the [Gemini page](/tools/gemini) for the app itself.

**Verdict:** worth trying for personal errands if you are in a supported country; not yet an option for work accounts.

## Microsoft Copilot Cowork and Autopilot

Copilot Cowork became generally available worldwide on June 16, 2026. It "executes complex, long-running, multi-tool tasks", and "cloud hosting means files are not stored locally … your tasks keep running even when your laptop is off." At Ignite on November 17, 2026, Microsoft began rolling out Autopilot, previously called Scout, to Frontier customers. "Each Autopilot has its own Microsoft Entra Agent ID and a named manager. IT can scope its access and block it anytime," and it "can check with you before key actions."

Control starts with the admin: "Cowork is off by default. Admins decide when to enable Cowork in their tenant." The limit is access: Copilot Cowork "requires the Microsoft 365 Copilot User Subscription License", and Cowork itself is billed by usage, so it is for Microsoft 365 organizations. See the [Microsoft 365 Copilot page](/tools/microsoft-copilot).

**Verdict:** the default for companies standardized on Microsoft 365, once IT turns it on.

## Cloud VM or your browser

The biggest difference between these agents is where the work happens. A cloud agent (Manus's sandbox, ChatGPT Work's cloud browser, Genspark, Perplexity Computer, Copilot Cowork) runs on a remote machine and reaches your accounts only through connectors or credentials you hand it; several say outright that work continues after you close the laptop or tab. An agent in your own browser (Comet, Manus Browser Operator, Tasks in Gemini on local Chrome) inherits every session you are signed into, which is convenient and is exactly what a malicious page would want. The [agentic browser](/glossary/agentic-browser) and [computer use](/glossary/computer-use) entries explain the mechanics.

Whichever you pick, put the approval at the step that sends, pays or deletes, not at the start of the task. The [human-in-the-loop workflows guide](/guides/workflow/human-in-the-loop-ai-workflows) shows where those checkpoints go.

## How to choose

- **Your work is documents, files and research.** Claude or ChatGPT Work, whichever your team already uses.
- **You want one standalone agent for cloud, browser and desktop.** Manus.
- **You want an agent inside the browser you browse with.** Perplexity Comet, kept away from banking and email.
- **You want recurring monitoring and research in the cloud.** Perplexity Computer or Manus Automations.
- **You want actions across SaaS apps, with approvals.** Lindy if you live in Slack; AI by Zapier if you already run Zaps.
- **You want a finished file from a single long job.** Genspark Super Agent, with each turn capped at two hours.
- **You use Gemini on a personal account.** Tasks in Gemini.
- **Your company runs on Microsoft 365.** Copilot Cowork, with Autopilot as it reaches Frontier customers.
