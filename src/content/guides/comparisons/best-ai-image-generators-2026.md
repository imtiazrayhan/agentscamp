For design work in 2026, pick an image generator by what it does after the first render: Ideogram if words appear in the artwork, Recraft if the deliverable is a vector or has to match a house style, Midjourney if the brief is a mood, Canva if the person generating is not a designer, and fal or Replicate if the generation happens inside a product you are building. Raw image quality has converged; text rendering, vector output, style control, and licensing have not. This page is the image chapter of [the best AI tools for designers in 2026](/guides/comparisons/best-ai-tools-for-designers-2026), and the [Claude Design guide](/guides/design/claude-design-guide) is the pillar behind both.

## Ideogram: when the words are the design

Ideogram built its reputation on rendering legible text, and in 2026 it went one step further. Generate a poster, book cover, or social graphic, press Layerize Text, and every line of text becomes a separate selectable layer you can rewrite, change the font of, or resize, with the visual design left as generated. Ideogram documents it as beta, notes that it works best on clear straight type rather than curved or decorative lettering, and makes it available on every plan including the free one, as well as through a Layerize endpoint in the API.

Around that sit styles, character consistency, custom model training on higher tiers, an ad resizer, a background remover, and an MCP server. Pricing is credit-based: a free tier with slow credits for eligible accounts, then Plus, Pro, Team, and Enterprise plans that add priority credits, private generation, and larger queues, with API usage billed separately. Ideogram also released quantized Ideogram 4.0 weights on Hugging Face under a non-commercial license with Apache-2.0 inference code, and sells a self-serve commercial license for self-hosting them.

## Recraft: the vector one

Recraft is the only tool here that generates genuinely editable vector graphics rather than pixels you have to trace, which is why it shows up in icon sets, logo exploration, and illustration systems. Custom styles are its second advantage: drop in a handful of your own images and Recraft turns them into a reusable style you can apply to everything after, which is a more reliable route to brand consistency than describing your look in a prompt. It also ships mockups, a photo editor, background removal, upscaling, and its own model line, plus an API and MCP server billed separately from subscription credits.

Read the plan boundary before you use it for work. Recraft's free plan is explicitly personal use only: generations are public, and there are no commercial rights. Commercial rights, private generation, third-party model families, and the advanced editing tools all start on the paid tiers.

## Midjourney: art direction, with terms attached

Midjourney is still where you go for a look. Style References are the mechanism that matters for designers: point at an image and Midjourney captures its palette, medium, texture, and lighting without copying its subject, then applies that across a set. Four subscription tiers, no free trial on the website or in Discord, and Stealth Mode, which keeps generations private, only on the top two.

The terms are the part people skip. Midjourney says you own the assets you create, with exceptions: upscales of another user's image stay theirs, and a company, or an employee of a company, grossing more than one million dollars a year must be on a Pro or Mega plan to own its assets. By default your content is publicly viewable and remixable, and Midjourney retains a perpetual license to the content you input and the assets you produce. For confidential client work, that combination is the decision.

## Canva: the generator everyone else is already using

Canva's generator is not aimed at you; it is aimed at the marketer, recruiter, and sales lead making assets without you. Text to Image, Text to Graphic, and Text to Video sit inside the editor with prebuilt styles, aspect ratios, and Magic Edit for local changes. Canva's AI terms say you retain ownership of your input and own your output, except where the output incorporates licensed library content, and that you may use your output for any lawful purpose. Usage is capped per plan tier, with top-ups when a team runs out. Treat Canva as the brand-governance surface it is: Brand Kits and approvals are the reason to care.

## fal and Replicate: models inside your product

When generation belongs in a product rather than a workflow, you rent a model instead of a seat. [fal](/tools/fal) is the generative-media specialist, billing per output unit or per compute on prepaid credits and hosting model families including Flux, Seedream, Nano Banana, GPT Image, and Qwen Image. [Replicate](/tools/replicate) runs a broader catalog of open models, billed per second of compute or per output, with Cog for packaging and serving your own fine-tune. Both are covered further in the [diffusion model](/glossary/diffusion-model) entry.

Licensing works differently here, and it is the thing to get right. Replicate's terms grant you all its right, title, and interest in the output, including commercial sale or publication, but explicitly subject to the third-party terms of the model you chose. fal's terms leave you owning your input, disclaim any warranty that an output is original or non-infringing, and forbid using third-party model outputs to train competing models. And an open-weight family is not automatically free to run: Black Forest Labs sells tiered commercial licenses for self-hosting FLUX, with image volumes, domain counts, and named-client limits per tier.

## How to choose

Start from the deliverable. Words in the artwork means Ideogram. A vector, icon, or repeatable house style means Recraft. A mood board or a campaign look means Midjourney, if the ownership terms fit your client work. Assets made by non-designers mean Canva with a locked Brand Kit. A generate button in your own product means fal or Replicate, with the model's license read before launch.
