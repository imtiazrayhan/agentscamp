**The best AI meeting note taker in 2026 depends on one question: do you want a bot in the call?** If not, [Granola](/tools/granola) is the answer; it captures from your device audio, works with any meeting app and stores no audio. If a visible recorder is fine, [Fathom](/tools/fathom) gives you unlimited free recordings with video, [Otter.ai](/tools/otter-ai) gives you the live transcript everyone can follow plus a layer of agents, and [Fireflies](/tools/fireflies) gives you the longest language list, the broadest integrations and the most established MCP server. tl;dv sits between them as a collaboration-first option, and the notetakers built into Zoom, Google Meet and Microsoft Teams are worth trying first if you already pay for the suite. Prices change often; each tool page lists current plans with dates.

## Granola: notes without a bot

Granola is a notepad app. You type rough notes during the meeting, it listens through your computer or phone audio, and afterwards it merges the two into structured notes. Nothing joins the call, which is why it works with Zoom, Google Meet, Teams "and every other meeting app" without an integration, and its security page says it does not store the audio at all. Since its May 2024 launch it has added Windows (June 2025), iOS, Android (July 2026) and Apple Watch (July 2026), Agentic Chat with inline citations across your meeting history, Briefs that prepare you before external meetings, and a first-party MCP connector for Claude, ChatGPT and Cursor.

The limits are the flip side of the design. There is no shared live transcript for other participants, the free plan keeps only 30 days of history, and the CRM integrations sit on paid tiers. Granola also writes publicly about the consent question it creates: it can post a message in the meeting chat when transcription begins, watermark your video, and it reminds you to ask every time you start.

**Verdict:** the best choice when you do not want a recorder in the room, and the cleanest tool for people who take their own notes anyway.

## Otter.ai: the shared live transcript

Otter's bot, Otter Notetaker, joins Zoom, Microsoft Teams and Google Meet as a named participant and posts a link to the live transcript in the chat. That live transcript is the product: everyone with the link can follow along, and the summary and action items arrive after. Otter layers more on top than anyone else here: briefings before a call, live coaching based on what has been said, chat across all your meetings, and agents for sales, SDR work (conversations with prospects through your website), recruiting (Greenhouse sync), education and media. The desktop app for Mac and Windows adds botless recording, and the pricing page lists an MCP server on the Pro plan.

The constraints are minute caps on the free and Pro plans, six transcription languages (English, Spanish, French, German, Japanese and Chinese), and Enterprise-only versions of the stronger consent tools such as pre-recording emails to every invitee.

**Verdict:** the pick when the live transcript and the agents matter more than language coverage, and you accept a visible participant.

## Fathom: unlimited free recording

Fathom's free plan includes unlimited recordings and transcriptions with instant summaries, which is the most generous free tier in this group. It records Zoom, Google Meet and Teams with a bot by default, produces clips and playlists, syncs to Slack, Salesforce, HubSpot, Notion and Asana, and answers questions through Ask Fathom. Version 3.0 added a per-meeting Capture Mode: Transcript Only (bot-free) keeps no recording, while Audio & Video (Bot) sends the bot. As of October 20, 2026 Fathom's help center says 3.0 and above is Mac-only, that Windows bot-free recording is in a limited Microsoft Teams alpha, and that bot-free video capture is a limited beta. The paid tiers are where the sales features live: global search and playlists on Team, CRM field sync, Deal View and AI scorecards on Business. Fathom's MCP connector works with Claude and ChatGPT and sees only meetings you can already view, and its help center lists English plus 19 other languages.

**Verdict:** the best free recorder, and the best fit for sales teams that want video and coaching; Windows users get the bot, not the bot-free modes.

## Fireflies: the integration surface

Fireflies is bot-first (its bot is Fred) and enterprise-shaped: the homepage claims use across more than a million companies, support for more than 100 languages, 200-plus AI Skills, and SOC 2 Type II and ISO 42001 badges. The desktop app for Windows and Mac adds bot-free capture, which Fireflies' own guide describes as system-audio capture with transcript and notes only. Its MCP server runs at api.fireflies.ai/mcp with OAuth, and Fireflies says it became the first AI meeting tool added to Anthropic's Claude connectors directory on August 21, 2025. A newer addition, Fireflies Talk, is free dictation anywhere you type. The [Fireflies page](/tools/fireflies) covers the detail, including that some AI features draw on credits separate from the seat.

**Verdict:** the choice for multilingual teams and anyone whose workflow already runs through many SaaS tools; the Claude connection is the best documented in the category.

## tl;dv: team collaboration, bot optional

tl;dv markets itself as "the AI notetaker built for team collaboration", with a free-forever plan, a "no bot required" badge, GDPR and SOC 2 claims and a stated two-million-plus user base. Its desktop app records Google Meet, Zoom and Teams directly from your device; tl;dv's own comparison pages describe that bot-free mode as audio-only. Integrations shown on the homepage are HubSpot, Slack and Notion. We have not reviewed tl;dv in the depth of the four tools above, so treat it as a candidate to trial rather than a verdict.

**Verdict:** worth a trial for teams in Europe that want a free plan and a bot-optional recorder, with less agentic machinery than Otter or Fireflies.

## The built-ins: Zoom, Google Meet and Microsoft Teams

### Zoom AI / My Notes (formerly AI Companion)

Zoom retired the AI Companion brand on June 24, 2026; its blog says "all the capabilities that used to carry the AI Companion name are still built right into Zoom Workplace, still included with eligible paid plans." The notetaker is My Notes, which Zoom says will "automatically take notes across Zoom, Teams, Google Meet, and other third-party meeting platforms, no bots required", with a visible indicator in Zoom meetings when transcription is active. Zoom describes a federated model approach that combines its own models with third-party ones from providers such as OpenAI. ZoomMate, generally available since June 1, 2026, is a separate paid agentic product that adds search, generated presentations and automated execution. If you already pay for Zoom and most meetings are internal, My Notes is the obvious first thing to try.

### Gemini in Google Meet

Google Meet's Take notes for me "requires an eligible Google Workspace edition or Google AI plan." It supports English, French, German, Italian, Japanese, Korean, Portuguese and Spanish, one language at a time, informs all participants that notes are being taken and shows a pencil icon, and saves the notes document to the organizer's Drive in a Google Meet folder. Google recommends it for meetings of 15 minutes to 8 hours and offers a "Summary so far" for late joiners; admins can require explicit consent. The [AI meeting notes workflow](/guides/workflow/ai-meeting-notes-action-items) explains why that Docs recap is a summary, not a transcript, and how to review it.

### Copilot in Microsoft Teams

Teams' intelligent recap is available with Teams Premium and with a Microsoft 365 Copilot license. It produces AI notes, tasks and timeline markers for name mentions, joins and leaves, and screen shares, and Microsoft says the content "is powered by GPT and other AI models." The event must be transcribed in a supported language (Microsoft lists 25) and be at least five minutes long, and recap is not currently available for ad-hoc events.

**Verdict on the built-ins:** right for single-platform internal meetings when you already pay for the suite; a standalone tool wins when you meet across platforms, need CRM sync or want Claude to query your meetings.

## Bot or no bot

The capture method decides more than people expect. A bot is honest by construction: it appears as a participant, usually with a chat message, and everyone can see it. The cost is social (some prospects and investors dislike it) and practical (bots get blocked by hosts, and they do not work for in-person conversations). Bot-free capture through device audio solves both, which is why Granola built its entire product on it, why Fathom, Otter, Fireflies and tl;dv added desktop apps that do it, and why Zoom's My Notes advertises "no bots required". The cost moves to you: without a visible bot, the notice has to come from your settings or your mouth. Granola's answer is an automatic chat message, a video watermark and a reminder to ask; Fathom's is a Recording Notice that admins can enforce; Otter's desktop app posts a Teams notification and shows a floating indicator.

Two further distinctions matter. First, what is kept: Granola keeps no audio, Fathom's Transcript Only mode saves no recording, and Fireflies' bot-free mode keeps transcript and notes only, while bot modes typically keep audio or video you can replay. That affects both privacy and how useful the record is when a summary is disputed. Second, who can follow along: only bot-based live transcripts give other participants a link during the call. ## How to choose

- **You do not want a bot, ever.** Granola.
- **You want free, unlimited recordings with video.** Fathom, with a Mac if you also want bot-free capture.
- **You want a live transcript everyone can follow, plus agents.** Otter.ai.
- **You meet in many languages or need many integrations.** Fireflies.
- **You want a free plan and a bot-optional recorder for a European team.** Trial tl;dv.
- **Your meetings are internal and on one platform you already pay for.** The built-in: Zoom AI's My Notes, Gemini in Meet, or Copilot in Teams.
- **You want Claude to answer questions across your meetings.** Any of Granola, Otter, Fathom or Fireflies through MCP; Fireflies' connector is the best documented.
- **You sell.** Fathom or Fireflies for coaching and CRM sync; [Claude for sales teams](/guides/sales/claude-for-sales-teams) shows what to do with the transcripts.
