**The best AI browser in 2026 is [Perplexity Comet](/tools/perplexity-comet) for most people**: it is free, it runs on Mac, Windows, iOS and Android, it keeps your Chrome extensions, and its assistant can read across your tabs and act. [Dia](/tools/dia-browser) is the pick when your work lives in Slack, Notion, Jira and Google Workspace and you want a browser that reads those apps rather than clicks for you. [Claude in Chrome](/tools/claude-for-chrome) is the pick for paid Claude users who want an agent inside the Chrome they already have. The assistants built into Chrome and Edge matter mainly for what they gate behind subscriptions and U.S. availability, and ChatGPT Atlas, the obvious comparison a year ago, stopped working on August 9, 2026. Prices are not quoted here; each tool page carries current plans with dates.

*Last reviewed: October 2026.*

An [agentic browser](/glossary/agentic-browser) is one where the assistant can read what you see and act on pages, not just chat beside them. Three shapes exist: new browsers built around the assistant (Comet, Dia, Opera Neon), assistants built into a browser you already use (Gemini in Chrome, Copilot in Edge, Brave Leo), and an extension that turns your existing browser into an agent (Claude in Chrome). The shape decides the switching cost; the agent mode decides the risk.

## Perplexity Comet: free, everywhere, and the most tested

Comet is a Chromium browser with Perplexity's answer engine in the address bar and an assistant in the sidebar; Perplexity's help center calls it "a Chromium-based browser that integrates advanced AI capabilities powered by Perplexity", with support for most Chrome extensions. The assistant reads the page, works across tabs and takes actions; Perplexity says you can see exactly what it is doing and step in at any time, and there is a pause control. It went from limited release on July 9, 2025 to free for everyone on October 2, 2025, then to Android in November 2025 and iOS in March 2026. Background Assistants, which run many agent tasks in parallel, and the Email Assistant need a Perplexity Max subscription. It is also the AI browser whose agent has been broken most publicly, partly because it was first and free; the prompt-injection section below has the detail.

**Verdict:** the default for most people. Free, cross-platform and capable, as long as you treat the agent as something you supervise.

## Dia: the work-app browser, now Atlassian's

Dia is the browser The Browser Company built after Arc, and since October 21, 2025 the company has been part of Atlassian. The sidebar reads your tabs and, with permission, your connected apps: Slack, Gmail, Google Calendar and Drive, Notion, Jira, Confluence, Granola, Loom, GitHub, Linear, Figma, Teams and more. A daily Morning Brief lays out calendar, inbox and key links; memory of your past activity is stored locally and can be disabled; Skills, Profiles, Splits and Decks round it out. It mixes "GPT (OpenAI Azure), Claude (Anthropic, Vertex, AWS), Gemini (Vertex)" with small on-device models choosing the provider. Dia is free on macOS and, since version 1.0 on October 6, 2026, on Windows, with paid usage-metered tiers rolling out to new Mac users first. It advertises no click-and-type agent mode as of October 2026; the model acts through connected-app tools and chat. Arc is in maintenance and "receives Chromium updates only".

**Verdict:** the pick for knowledge work scattered across SaaS tabs, and the one to choose if you want the context without the cursor-driving risk.

## Claude in Chrome: an agent inside the Chrome you have

Anthropic's extension, renamed from Claude for Chrome, "reads the page you're signed in to, then clicks, types, and fills forms". It became generally available on every paid Claude plan on August 26, 2026, after a pilot with 1,000 Max users from August 2025. Anthropic says "a safety classifier validates each action before it's performed", and its support article lists scheduled tasks, multi-tab work, a 1Password sign-in integration on macOS and admin allowlists on Team and Enterprise. It does not run on other Chromium browsers or on mobile. Anthropic publishes the most detailed red-team figures of any vendor here: with probes and the classifier, "no attacks succeeded against Claude Sonnet 5, Claude Opus 5, or Claude Mythos 5", against a 3.8% success rate for Opus 5 without them. Those are Anthropic's numbers, not independent ones.

**Verdict:** the best way to get a browser agent without changing browsers, if you already pay for Claude; the paid-only requirement and desktop-only Chrome are the limits.

## Gemini in Chrome: built in, with auto browse for subscribers

Google put Gemini into Chrome for Mac and Windows users in the U.S. in September 2025, and in January 2026 introduced Chrome auto browse "for AI Pro and Ultra subscribers in the U.S.", built on Gemini 3, alongside a new side panel and Connected Apps. Google says auto browse "is designed to pause and explicitly ask for your confirmation" before actions like making a purchase. Chrome's AI features are rolling out to select Android devices in the U.S., but Google's support page says auto browse "is only available on desktop" and requires a personal Google AI Pro or Ultra account.

**Verdict:** the zero-effort option if you are in the U.S. and already on Google AI Pro or Ultra; elsewhere it is a chat side panel, not an agent.

## Copilot in Edge: Copilot Mode is gone, Browse with Copilot is Premium-only

Microsoft launched Copilot Mode in Edge in July 2025 and retired it on May 13, 2026 ("As part of today's update, we're retiring Copilot Mode"). The features live on as Copilot in Edge, with multi-tab reasoning, history-aware answers, Journeys (now free in all English markets), long-term memory on desktop and mobile, and Vision and Voice on desktop and in the Edge mobile app. The agentic part, formerly Copilot Actions, "is now available as Browse with Copilot on Edge desktop for Microsoft 365 Premium Subscribers in the U.S. only."

**Verdict:** fine as a built-in assistant for Edge users; the agent is gated behind Microsoft 365 Premium and U.S. availability.

## Opera Neon: subscription-only and built to act

Opera Neon is the one browser here that is pay-to-use from the start: "Subscribe to Neon to activate agentic functionalities", with no free tier. Opera announced it in May 2025, ran a closed Founders phase from October 2, 2025, and opened it to anyone on December 11, 2025, bundling models it listed at the time as Gemini 3 Pro, GPT-5.1, Veo 3.1 and Nano Banana Pro. The product is organised around Chat, Do and Make agents, a deep-research agent, and workflows "through MCP and CLI paths". Opera's own security note says "the risk of a successful prompt injection attack cannot be entirely reduced to zero", so it blacklists banking sites and keeps a human in the loop. The pages we read do not list platforms.

**Verdict:** for people who want a browser that is primarily an agent runner and will pay for it; everyone else should start with Comet.

## Brave Leo: privacy first, agent behind a flag

Brave Leo is the assistant built into Brave on macOS, Windows, Linux, Android and iOS. Brave says Leo "doesn't retain or share chats, or use them for additional model training", needs no account, is free with a Premium tier, and lets you bring your own local or remote models. Agentic browsing is a cautious, separate story: Brave's post says "AI browsing is currently behind an opt-in feature flag" and "happens only in an isolated browsing profile", in early testing across release channels as of May 2026. Brave is also the source of the most-cited independent research on this category's security.

**Verdict:** the privacy choice, and the broadest platform list; not yet the choice if you want an agent today.

## What happened to ChatGPT Atlas

OpenAI launched ChatGPT Atlas on macOS on October 21, 2025, with browser memories, an agent mode for paid users, and Windows, iOS and Android "coming soon". Those versions never shipped. On July 9, 2026 OpenAI's release notes said: "We're deprecating Atlas as we bring browser-based agentic capabilities into ChatGPT and Codex. Atlas is scheduled to stop working on August 9, 2026." Its replacements, in OpenAI's words: "For deeper browser-based agentic work, try the ChatGPT desktop app. For help while browsing in Chrome, try the ChatGPT Chrome extension or sidebar, where available." OpenAI's help center now says "ChatGPT Atlas has been retired and no longer receives updates", and chatgpt.com/atlas loads the ordinary ChatGPT page. If Atlas was on your shortlist, Comet is the nearest full browser and Claude in Chrome or OpenAI's extension the nearest agent inside Chrome.

## The prompt-injection problem

An assistant that reads pages and acts on them can be instructed by the page. That is [prompt injection](/glossary/prompt-injection), and in this category it is not theoretical.

- **Brave on Comet, August 20, 2025.** Hidden instructions in a Reddit comment made Comet read the user's Perplexity email and a Gmail one-time code.
- **Brave on Comet, October 21, 2025.** "Malicious instructions embedded as nearly-invisible text within the image are processed as commands."
- **Brave on Opera Neon, October 31, 2025.** Hidden text styled with zero opacity leaked the user's Opera account email; Opera confirmed a fix on October 21, 2025.
- **Trail of Bits on Comet, February 20, 2026.** Its pre-launch audit in April 2025 found "four prompt injection techniques could extract users' private information from Gmail".
- **Dia's own bulletin.** The team found its `fetch_web_content` feature "could be exploited for data exfiltration via prompt injection", removed it before the June 2025 public launch, rebuilt it with architectural controls, and wrote that "our team was able to bypass every LLM-based detection mechanism we tried".

The vendors' responses converge: classifiers that screen page content (Perplexity's four-layer defence and open-sourced BrowseSafe; Anthropic's per-action safety classifier), confirmation before consequential actions (Google's auto browse, Claude in Chrome), isolated profiles (Brave), blocked site categories (Opera) and the admission that it is not solved. OpenAI wrote that prompt injection "is unlikely to ever be fully 'solved'" and Opera that the risk "cannot be entirely reduced to zero". The practical rules: run agent features in a separate profile with only the accounts a task needs, keep them off banking, email and password managers, prefer read-only tasks, and watch. [Defending against prompt injection](/guides/ai-safety/defending-prompt-injection) covers the engineering side, and [how computer-use agents work](/guides/concepts/how-computer-use-agents-work) explains why a hostile page is both the agent's input and its instruction field.

## How to choose

- **A free AI browser on any device.** Comet.
- **Your work is in Slack, Notion, Jira and Google Workspace.** Dia.
- **You pay for Claude and want an agent in your existing Chrome.** Claude in Chrome.
- **You are in the U.S. on Google AI Pro or Ultra.** Gemini in Chrome with auto browse.
- **You are an Edge user on Microsoft 365 Premium in the U.S.** Browse with Copilot.
- **You want a browser that is mostly an agent runner and will pay.** Opera Neon.
- **Privacy first, Linux included, no agent needed yet.** Brave Leo.
- **You are a developer automating a workflow.** Read [browser agents compared](/guides/comparisons/browser-agents-compared-2026) and start with [Browser Use](/tools/browser-use).
