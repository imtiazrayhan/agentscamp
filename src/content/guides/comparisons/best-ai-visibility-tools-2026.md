The best AI visibility tool in 2026 depends on who is buying. [Profound](/tools/profound) is the enterprise pick: nine engines, server-log bot analytics, ChatGPT Shopping tracking, and a hosted MCP server, on custom contracts. [Peec AI](/tools/peec-ai) is the self-serve pick: daily tracking, unlimited users, and an MCP server on the product. Ahrefs Brand Radar has the most candid methodology page, Semrush folds AI visibility into the suite SEO teams already pay for, Otterly and Scrunch sit between self-serve and enterprise, AthenaHQ has the widest model list and a free tier, and [Surfer](/tools/surfer) and [Clearscope](/tools/clearscope) add tracking to content suites. All nine run prompts through answer engines, record whether a brand appears, and report which sources the answers cite; the [AI visibility](/glossary/ai-visibility) entry defines the metric. Prices are left to the tool pages.

## Profound: the enterprise standard

Profound "runs structured prompts across AI platforms, analyzing where and how your brand appears in responses." Enterprise tracks up to nine engines daily, including Perplexity, Claude, Copilot, DeepSeek, and Exa Search. Around it sit FactCheck ("shows you inaccurate claims and their sources"), Prompt Volumes drawn from "millions of conversations," and Agent Analytics read from CDN and host logs. Enterprise adds ChatGPT Shopping tracking at SKU level, a REST API, and a hosted MCP server; the homepage lists SOC 2, RBAC, and SCIM. The trial is free for seven days on three engines; after that pricing is custom.

**Verdict:** the enterprise pick when you need a security review, a shopping catalog, or engines beyond the usual five, and can sign a custom contract.

## Peec AI: the self-serve tracker

Peec AI "runs your prompts across AI platforms like ChatGPT, Gemini, and Copilot daily, then analyzes patterns over time," reporting Visibility, Position, and Sentiment, with the cited source as the unit of analysis. Entry tiers choose three of six models (ChatGPT, Google AI Mode, AI Overviews, Copilot, Gemini, Naver AI) with 50, 150, or 350 tracked prompts; Enterprise opens up to 13 including Claude, Perplexity, Grok, and DeepSeek. Every plan has unlimited users. Prompt volume is "relative demand for the topic behind each tracked prompt, scored 1 to 5," not a count. Crawl Insights reads server logs, and the MCP server connects to Claude, Cursor, and n8n. The company is Peec AI GmbH in Berlin.

**Verdict:** the self-serve pick for a team or agency that wants daily tracking, unlimited seats, and an MCP server without a sales cycle.

## Otterly.ai: seven engines and the full answers

Otterly tracks seven engines: Google AI Overviews, ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Copilot as standard, with AI Mode, Gemini, and Claude as add-ons; Claude is tracked "via API, using Haiku model with web search capabilities." Prompts run "on a daily or weekly schedule," and Otterly "stores the full generated response each time." Citation tracking lists "every cited URL, whether it names you, and which rival it names instead." In 2026 it shipped a public API (May), an MCP server (June), and server-log Agent Analytics (August). Volume is an explicit proxy: "OpenAI doesn't disclose Prompt Volume data directly. That's why we built an Estimated Intent Score."

**Verdict:** the mid-market pick for more engines than Surfer or Clearscope, an API and MCP, and stored answers, without Profound's contract.

## Semrush AI Visibility Toolkit: the suite extension

Semrush, an Adobe company since April 28, 2026, bundles its AI Visibility Toolkit with the SEO toolkit as Semrush One (October 29, 2025). It covers Google AI Overviews, AI Mode, ChatGPT, Gemini, and Perplexity, built on "over 317 million prompts and responses" that are "captured from real requests and not via any APIs of LLMs." Prompt Tracking runs 25 of your own prompts daily on ChatGPT, AI Mode, and Gemini with cited domains. Topic-level volume combines "third-party data on real AI interactions with Semrush's machine learning models," and the method is documented on a knowledge-base page, which is rare. Site Audit flags blocked AI crawlers but there is no log analytics; the MCP server is read-only. Claude and Copilot are not named.

**Verdict:** the pick when the team already works in Semrush and wants AI visibility next to rankings, with the biggest prompt database and the narrowest engine list.

## Ahrefs Brand Radar: the documented one

Brand Radar has an AI Visibility Index ("any brand's AI visibility instantly across 493 million prompts. No setup") and Custom Prompts that run daily, weekly, or monthly. The October 29, 2025 methodology post is the most explicit in the category: prompts come from People Also Ask questions, "all prompts run through the free, publicly available web interfaces of ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Copilot," "no normalization, personalization, pre-prompting, or filtering is applied," the index refreshes monthly over a 90-day window, and "we do not filter out hallucinated or malformed links, as they reflect real model output." On volume, Estimated Impressions weight mentions by Google search volume, and "this is a modeling choice, not a measured relationship." Bot Analytics reads CDN logs, and data flows through the API and remote MCP server.

**Verdict:** the pick for SEO-led teams that want an instant, explainable index and accept a monthly refresh instead of daily runs of their own prompts.

## Surfer AI Tracker: tracking next to the editor

Surfer's AI Tracker covers "ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Mode, Google AI Overview, and Google Gemini," daily on Pro and above or 25 prompts on ChatGPT weekly on Standard. It "scrape[s] the real-world interfaces your customers actually see," repeats prompts and averages them, and reports visibility, mention rate, position, and sources. The MCP server left beta in October 2026 for Pro and above; the API is Enterprise-only; and Agent Analytics, which "reads your server logs," was in closed beta as of October 2026. Claude is not named, and there is no volume metric.

**Verdict:** the pick when the team already writes in Surfer and wants brand tracking wired into the editor.

## Clearscope Query Tracking: the content team's add-on

Clearscope launched Query Tracking on July 14, 2026 with "seamless monitoring across the big three AI models, ChatGPT, Gemini, and Claude." You add your domain and up to five competitors, and each query reports a mention percentage as "a smoothed, daily rolling average of the 21 most recent responses." Essentials includes 50 tracked queries and Business 300, with unlimited users. Missing: Google's AI surfaces, cited-URL reports, bot analytics, volume estimates, and any API or MCP documentation we could find.

**Verdict:** the pick for a content team in Clearscope that wants a cheap, smoothed mention rate on three chat models.

## Scrunch: personas and geographies

Scrunch, "a Sitecore company" since June 3, 2026, is the enterprise-leaning alternative to Profound. Core plans cover ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews, and Copilot; Enterprise covers nine, adding Claude, Gemini, Meta AI, AI Mode, and Grok, though "Grok no longer contributes citation data." Data is gathered "including browser automation and official platform APIs," daily for fourteen days and then on "a default 72-hour refresh cadence." The differentiator is segmentation: Scrunch "uniquely supports persona and geography-based tracking and automatically generates relevant prompts for each segment." Agent Traffic breaks down visits by assistant, a Query API and MCP wrap the metrics, and the Keyword Volume Estimator, which scales "a large panel of real consumer AI conversations" up to the US population, was in beta for all customers as of October 2026.

**Verdict:** the pick for large brands that need persona and geography segmentation from one Sitecore-backed vendor.

## AthenaHQ: the widest model list, with a free tier

AthenaHQ, a Y Combinator Winter 2025 company, lists "visibility across 11 models," including Grok, DeepSeek, Meta AI, and Mistral. The free Essential plan includes 300 credits, one per AI response. Prompt volume comes from a proprietary Query Volume Estimation Model whose explainer claims a "95%+ accuracy rate" without published validation, and no page describes how prompts are run, how often, or from where. The MCP server takes OAuth 2.0 or an API key and ships a ChatGPT plugin; API access is an add-on on Starter. There is no bot-traffic analytics, only robots.txt and llms.txt management.

**Verdict:** the pick for a small team that wants the longest engine list and a free start, trading documented method for recommendations.

## What these tools can and cannot measure

Every number here comes from running a prompt and reading the reply, and replies move. Peec's docs say "AI responses naturally vary day to day," Clearscope smooths with a 21-response rolling average, Surfer repeats and averages, Scrunch drops to 72-hour refreshes after two weeks, and Ahrefs refreshes monthly. A one-day dip is noise; a four-week trend is signal.

How the prompt is run matters as much as how often: Ahrefs and Surfer read public web interfaces, Semrush captures real requests, Otterly reaches Claude through the API, and an API answer and a consumer answer can cite different sources, so two tools will disagree and neither is wrong. Who writes the prompts matters too: most tools track prompts you choose, Ahrefs and Semrush start from search-derived questions, and Scrunch generates prompts per persona and geography.

Prompt volume is a proxy everywhere. No engine discloses it, and the candid vendors say so: Otterly's Estimated Intent Score, Ahrefs' "modeling choice," Semrush's topic volumes, Scrunch's scaled panel, Peec's one-to-five score. AthenaHQ's accuracy claim and Profound's "millions of conversations" are vendor statements without a public method. Use the scores to order your own prompts, not to size a market.

Engine lists are plan-gated: Claude is an add-on at Otterly, Custom-Prompts-only at Ahrefs, Enterprise-only at Peec and Scrunch, and unnamed at Surfer and Semrush. Bot analytics and crawler checks differ: five tools read logs; Semrush and AthenaHQ only tell you whether robots.txt blocks crawlers. And hallucinated citations are real output: Ahrefs keeps malformed links because the model produced them.

## How to choose

An enterprise brand with a security questionnaire and a product catalog should shortlist Profound (nine engines, SOC 2 Type II, SCIM, shopping tracking, hosted MCP) and Scrunch (persona and geography segmentation). Both need a sales conversation.

A marketing team of three to thirty, or an agency, should start with Peec AI or Otterly: self-serve, daily runs, server-log bot analytics, API plus MCP. Peec adds unlimited users and a German legal entity; Otterly adds stored answers and seven engines. [AI content and search in 2026](/guides/marketing/ai-content-and-search-2026) explains what to change once the reports show a gap.

An SEO team already paying for Semrush or Ahrefs should turn on the toolkit or Brand Radar first, and add a specialist only for daily runs of its own prompts on engines the suite does not name. A content team should let the editor decide between Surfer's AI Tracker and Clearscope's Query Tracking, both weighed in [Surfer vs Clearscope](/guides/comparisons/surfer-vs-clearscope). A small team should start free with AthenaHQ, Semrush's free plan, or the Ahrefs and Semrush checkers.

Whatever you pick, wire it to an agent: eight of the nine ship MCP servers, so the weekly review can be a prompt rather than a dashboard session; the [SEO content workflow with Claude Code](/guides/marketing/seo-content-workflow-with-claude-code) guide shows the pattern.
