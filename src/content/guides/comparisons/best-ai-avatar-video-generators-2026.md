## HeyGen: the broadest platform

HeyGen's newest model, Avatar 6, arrived in November 2026; HeyGen says it "understands your script and performs it the way you want," renders "native 2K," gestures to match the words, and can put two avatars in one frame. Paid plans list 700+ stock presenters, and a digital twin trains on 15 to 600 seconds of footage (about two minutes recommended), cloning the voice from the same recording. A photo avatar needs only one image.

Translation is HeyGen's strongest suit: voice cloning and lip-sync across 175+ languages on paid plans, in a fast Speed mode or a Precision mode with editable proofread sessions before the final render. Developers get the v3 API (v1 and v2 reached end of support on October 31, 2026), a CLI, an MCP server and an MIT-licensed skills repository; the Business plan adds SCORM export and LMS integrations. Real-time, conversational avatars now live in LiveAvatar, a separate HeyGen product with its own platform and credits.

**Verdict:** the default for marketing, sales and localization, and the one to choose when developers will automate it.

## Synthesia: built for enterprise training

Synthesia's Express-3 model, launched July 15, 2026, is "our most advanced avatar model for lip sync, natural body movement and gestures," available on all plans. Stock avatars come from actors who gave consent. Personal avatars start from a photo on paid plans or, the legacy way, a 1 to 5-minute single take, and Studio avatars need three 2 to 3-minute recordings. The platform offers "over 140 languages and voices," AI dubbing that preserves the speaker's own voice, with lip sync at twice the credits, and one-click translation on Business.

What training buyers notice is the paperwork. SCORM export packages a course "with all translated versions," Synthesia reports ISO 42001, ISO 27001 and SOC 2 Type II audits, and customer data is stored in the EU. Interactive Avatars, real-time avatars that hold a live conversation, are in beta as of November 2026, and Syren, an AI video agent that writes a video as code and renders it, is labelled new on the pricing page.

**Verdict:** the pick for training and internal communications at companies that ask about consent records, audits and data residency first.

## Colossyan: the L&D alternative

Colossyan is built around courses. Its pricing page says it "supports 120+ languages," with avatar narration, on-screen text and interactive elements localizing together "with lip-sync in every language." It offers 300+ stock avatars on its NEO 2 model, instant custom avatars from about 20 seconds of footage or from a single photo or video, and Studio avatars that take about five working days. A custom avatar speaks 30+ languages in your own voice and 120+ with stock voices. Courses (Colossyan Learn), interactive branching and quizzes, and conversational avatars for real-time sessions round it out.

SCORM is the draw for training teams: Professional includes five SCORM exports a month, and Enterprise includes unlimited SCORM "compatible with any SCORM 1.2 or 2004 LMS," naming Workday, Cornerstone, Docebo, Litmos and Absorb. Colossyan is SOC 2 Type II certified, with EU or US data residency on Enterprise. The catch for developers: "API video generation is available on the NEO 1 model only."

**Verdict:** the pick for L&D teams that want course structure, branching and SCORM in one tool and do not need the API on the newest avatars.

## D-ID: developers and real time

D-ID's custom avatars come from at least one minute of footage on its V3 Instant model, and it now offers V4 Expressive avatars. Its homepage says it "supports video creation and real-time interactions in 120+ languages," while its pricing page lists 30+ output languages for Video Translate on every plan, so check that your target languages are covered. The API covers real-time streaming as well as rendered video, and D-ID launched what it calls Agentic Videos on April 23, 2026.

Its consent flow is documented step by step. Consent "must be recorded via webcam — uploads are not allowed for security reasons," a dynamic passcode appears on screen and must be read aloud, and D-ID checks the transcript, matches the face against the avatar footage, and verifies the voice. Through the API, you upload a recording of the subject reading the consent text instead, and the consent challenge expires 30 minutes after it is created.

**Verdict:** the pick when avatars are a feature inside your own product, especially conversational ones, and you want consent verification you can point to.

The homepage claims 100+ languages, the app translates videos with subtitles or a dub, and the twin can switch languages. **Verdict:** the smallest footage requirement here, suited to creators and social teams who want a twin of themselves.

Both editors now ship avatars, which changes the advice for teams that only need a presenter occasionally. VEED has its own model: "Fabric 1.0 is VEED's proprietary model for creating affordable, AI lip sync videos from text or an image and audio file." Its avatar tool offers 60+ stock avatars or a digital clone of you, recorded from a pre-set script and ready "in just a few hours," and its avatars speak 120+ languages. Fabric 1.0 is also sold as an API. We found no published consent procedure for VEED's custom avatars.

Descript lets you "create your avatar from a photo, a text prompt, or Descript's stock gallery," assign it to a speaker, and have it lip-sync to your audio. The gallery has 35+ avatars, prompts must describe "a realistic, adult, non-celebrity, human-like figure," and uploaded photos may be rejected if they contain celebrities, minors or explicit content. Descript also translates and dubs video in 30+ languages with a proofread step.

**Verdict:** enough when the presenter is a small part of a video you are editing anyway; for avatar-led video at volume, use a dedicated platform.

## Consent, deepfakes and the EU AI Act

An avatar of a real person reproduces their face and usually their voice, so the consent check is the most important feature on this list. The vendors differ:

- **Synthesia:** consent "must be recorded live and cannot be uploaded," by the same person shown in the photo; Studio performers read a consent script in their native language; owners can ask for their data to be deleted.
- **D-ID:** webcam-only consent in its web app (its API takes an uploaded recording instead, submitted within 30 minutes of the consent challenge), a passcode read aloud, and transcript, face and voice checks.
- **HeyGen:** the person records a consent video reading HeyGen's text, including a code; Enterprise accounts can upload one instead or skip consent after signing an indemnity agreement, and photo avatars get "no consent check."
- **Colossyan:** "explicit consent," requesters must be at least 18, and no avatars "of minors or animals."
- **Descript:** prompts must describe a non-celebrity adult, and photos of celebrities or minors may be rejected.
- **Captions and VEED:** no published procedure found.

The law now has a say in the EU. The AI Act defines a deep fake as "AI-generated or manipulated image, audio or video content that resembles existing persons, objects, places, entities or events and would falsely appear to a person to be authentic or truthful." Under Article 50(4), "Deployers of an AI system that generates or manipulates image, audio or video content constituting a deep fake, shall disclose that the content has been artificially generated or manipulated," and Article 50(5) says this must happen "at the latest at the time of the first interaction or exposure." Under Article 50(2), providers "shall ensure that the outputs of the AI system are marked in a machine-readable format and detectable as artificially generated or manipulated."

Article 50 applies from 2 August 2026. Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, the Digital Omnibus on AI, gives providers whose systems were placed on the market before 2 August 2026 until 2 December 2026 to comply with Article 50(2). It did not change the deepfake disclosure duty in Article 50(4). Synthesia's own blog footer already says its content "is labeled in accordance with EU AI Act Article 50 requirements." The practical rule: if a viewer could take your avatar for real footage of a real person, tell them it is AI-generated no later than the moment they first see it. This is not legal advice.

## Which should you pick

### Training and L&D

Start with [Synthesia](/tools/synthesia) if the course has to ship in several languages to an LMS: its SCORM export includes every translated version, and its audits and EU data storage answer procurement questions early. Colossyan is the alternative when you want branching, quizzes and course structure in the same tool. [HeyGen](/tools/heygen) Business covers SCORM and LMS integrations too, and makes sense if the same team also produces marketing video.

### Marketing and sales personalization

A video per prospect or per segment is a rendering job you repeat, so pick a tool with an API: HeyGen offers a v3 API, a CLI and an MCP server, and Synthesia, D-ID, Mirage and VEED's Fabric 1.0 all sell API access. Keep the script short and written for speech; the [avatar video script writer](/skills/marketing/avatar-video-script-writer) skill is built for that step. For social clips of yourself, Captions' twin, made from 10 seconds of footage, is the lightest start.

### Localization

HeyGen translates existing video with voice cloning and lip-sync across 175+ languages, and its Precision mode adds a proofread step. Synthesia dubs in the speaker's own voice, with lip sync at extra cost, and Colossyan localizes narration, on-screen text and interactions together. Descript's dubbing in 30+ languages with proofread is enough for occasional work. Whatever you use, review the target against the source before publishing: the [AI localization review workflow](/guides/marketing/ai-localization-review-workflow) sets up the check, and the [localization copy reviewer](/agents/marketing/localization-copy-reviewer) agent flags lost meaning, negation and numbers for a human reviewer.

### Real-time conversation

HeyGen's LiveAvatar runs real-time avatars in FULL mode, where HeyGen manages speech recognition, the LLM and speech synthesis, or LITE mode with your own agent. Synthesia's Interactive Avatars, in beta as of November 2026, run on [LiveKit](/tools/livekit), D-ID streams real-time avatars through its API, and Colossyan offers conversational avatars.
